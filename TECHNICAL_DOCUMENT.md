# Ball possession from automatic tracking: technical document

More than a Hack 2027, Phase 1 -- FC Barcelona vs Athletic Club. Submission: one label per frame
of the tactical video (H, A, D), generated by `solution/pipeline.py`.

## 1. Approach in one paragraph

Possession is a sequence of long, sticky states, so it is decoded rather than classified frame by
frame. Per frame we turn the tracking into evidence for "Barcelona has it", "Athletic has it" and
"dead ball": who is nearest the ball (weighted up at touches, down while the ball is in flight),
whether players have slowed to a walk, whether the ball is out of the pitch or standing still. A
hidden Markov model with football-derived stay probabilities finds the most likely H/A/D sequence
(Viterbi). Three models built on this evidence -- the HMM, a last-touch state machine, and the HMM
with a self-supervised team-shape model -- vote per frame; short blips are merged away and each
boundary is snapped to the nearest supporting event (a touch, the ball leaving the pitch).

## 2. Results

spellF1 is the ranking metric (possession spells matched by temporal IoU 0.3-0.7), macroF1 the
tie-break. "Validation" is 10 minutes we labelled by hand from the video (31 possessions, §6);
"combined" is that plus the public 5 minutes (48 possessions). Intervals are 95% bootstraps.

| | public spellF1 | validation spellF1 | combined spellF1 | combined macroF1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Submission (ensemble)** | 0.555 | 0.393 | **0.448** [0.34-0.56] | **0.818** |
| HMM alone | 0.542 | 0.328 | 0.396 | 0.782 |
| Organisers' baseline, smooth50 | 0.326 | 0.210 | 0.244 | 0.609 |
| Organisers' baseline, smooth125 | 0.424 | 0.313 | 0.349 | 0.551 |

smooth50 scores 0.243 on the private full match; it scores 0.244 on our combined set, which
suggests the combined set is a fair stand-in. The public window is easier than the rest of the
match for every system, so we expect our full-match spellF1 nearer the combined figure than the
public one.

## 4. Possession rules implemented

From the challenge definition (`CHALLENGE.md`), as the code expresses them:

- **Three states.** Every graded frame is H, A or D; no abstentions are submitted.
- **Change of possession = a touch with control by the other side.** Touches are detected where the
  ball's velocity changes by more than 5 m/s within ~0.25 s with a player within 2.5 m; the
  nearest player's side gets 4x evidence there. A fast ball (> 8 m/s) with nobody near is in flight
  and its nearest player gets 0.3x: deflections and passes in the air do not hand the ball over.
- **Loose balls stay with the last team** through the HMM's stickiness: switching needs sustained
  evidence. The last-touch member makes this explicit: two opposing touches are needed to hand the
  ball over.
- **Dead ball** when the players' median speed drops to a walk (1.1 m/s, from positions), the ball
  is out of the pitch, or the ball is still for a second while visible. Halftime and the time
  before the second-half kickoff are D.
- **Restarts:** after a stoppage the side that touches first has the ball; boundaries are snapped to
  that touch.

## 5. Missing and noisy tracking

- **The ball is missing on 41% of graded frames, but mostly while play is stopped:** during play it
  is missing on only 6%. Gaps up to 0.5 s are interpolated; longer gaps carry no team evidence, so
  the HMM keeps the current possession. Missing ball is weak evidence for D, never decisive.
- **No player identities, 46 track slots for 22 players.** Everything is collective: nearest side,
  median speeds (robust to identity swaps; per-slot jumps above 11 m/s are dropped), team centroid
  and spread.
- **The ATD's own speed field** labelled most of open play as stoppage when used for D, so speeds
  are computed from positions instead.
- **Correlated frames.** Consecutive frames are not independent; log evidence is divided by a
  temper (6 frames, 3 for the shape member) so a single touch is not counted 25 times a second.
- **Broadcast tracking** sees a median of 15 players against 21. We synced it piecewise without
  the ball (30 s windows placed by one-sided nearest-player distance, grown from anchors, with
  frame-level and ball-agreement checks): 38.7% of broadcast frames synced, but the broadcast loses
  the ball at the same moments as the tactical camera, so ball coverage rose only 56.9% -> 58.1%
  and scores did not change. Dropped from the submission (`--broadcast` keeps it).

## 6. How settings were chosen (and what we did not do)

- **Never fitted to the public window.** 17 possessions cannot rank variants (its bootstrap
  interval is ±0.18 spellF1); the organisers' own baselines show that the best public setting
  loses on the full match.
- **Full-match plausibility without ground truth.** The two main knobs were set so the full
  match looks like football: the walking threshold so ~44% of graded frames are dead ball (typical
  LaLiga ball-out-of-play time), the temper so possessions average ~14 s.
- **Hand-labelled validation.** We labelled 10 minutes outside the public window (minutes 25-30
  and 95-100 of the video) after calibrating against the public window (macroF1 0.945 against the
  official labels). They were used only to validate and to choose among five ensemble candidates
  fixed in advance -- never to train a model, and the submitted labels all come from the pipeline.
- **Candidates were accepted only if better on more than one test** and plausible on the full
  match. Rejected along the way, all kept as options: Smart Tagging priors, an Erlang duration
  model, a local logistic model trained between same-side touches, line-exit and sticky-out-of-play
  dead-ball evidence, faster distance fading, a 5-way vote, broadcast filling.
  `solution/ROADMAP.md` lists every attempt with its numbers.

## 7. Limitations

- **Team assignment in contested play** is the main error: about 9% of in-play frames go to the
  wrong side, peaking at 14% when the ball is 2-5 m from everyone (loose balls).
- **Dead-ball timing:** a quarter of the dead-ball frames are called in play, usually with the
  ball visible, because players are still moving just after the ball goes out.
- **Validation is small.** 48 possessions give a spellF1 interval of about ±0.11, and the 10
  hand-labelled minutes were also used to choose between candidates, so they are not an untouched
  test.
- **Built for one match.** The settings are football priors, not fitted to this match, but nothing
  has been tested on a second game.

## 8. Reproduce

    uv venv --python 3.12 .venv
    uv pip install --python .venv -r requirements-solution.txt
    # unpack the data into ./data (or set MTAH_DATA_DIR), then:
    .venv/Scripts/python.exe solution/pipeline.py --name <team_name>
    .venv/Scripts/python.exe evaluation/validate_submission.py evaluation/results/<team_name>.csv

Deterministic: no randomness, so the file is identical on every run. About 25 s. The code is
numpy + pandas only.

| file | role |
|---|---|
| `solution/pipeline.py` | the submission, end to end |
| `solution/hmm_possession.py` | evidence + HMM (also runnable alone) |
| `solution_alt/last_touch.py`, `common.py`, `ensemble.py` | last-touch member, shared inputs, vote |
| `solution/broadcast_sync.py` | broadcast syncing (not in the submission) |
| `tools/experiment.py`, `tools/make_validation_gt.py`, `validation/README.md` | evaluation and hand-labelling workflow |

## 9. Use of an LLM

The code was written with an AI coding assistant (Claude Code). In line with the competition
terms, the match data never reached it: every script that reads the data ran through a wrapper
(`tools/run_blind.py`) that hides all output, enforced by a hook, and the assistant judged results
only through the official scorer and validator, plus aggregate counts.
